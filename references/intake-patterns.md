# Intake Patterns

## Purpose

Help the user define the accompaniment direction quickly without forcing a long questionnaire.

## Minimal intake

Use this when the user is ready to move fast:

1. What artist or 1-2 songs are the closest references?
2. Do you want this more intimate, cinematic, glossy, or raw?
3. Is the goal a writing demo, a near-finished backing track, or a Logic-ready sketch?

## Producer-style intake prompts

- “If we build this around late-night energy, should it feel expensive or vulnerable?”
- “Does the chorus need to bloom, or stay controlled?”
- “Should the drums feel pocketed and understated, or more present and radio-ready?”
- “Do you want the harmony to feel seductive, melancholic, or triumphant?”
- “Are we leaving space for topline later, or should the arrangement already feel full?”

## Turning vague answers into production choices

If the user says:

- “night drive”
  Translate toward: moody keys, restrained drums, sub bass, selective shimmer

- “radio”
  Translate toward: cleaner hook lift, stronger top-end contrast, clearer chorus identity

- “SZA / Brent / alt-R&B”
  Translate toward: less clutter, more air, softer transient profile, intimacy over maximalism

- “The Weeknd / cinematic”
  Translate toward: stronger drama, brighter synth framing, more defined section lifts

## Good follow-up questions

Use only one or two if still unclear:

- “Should I optimize for emotion or replay value first?”
- “Do you want the chorus to sound bigger, or just closer?”
- “Should I keep this sparse for toplining?”
